### Freese's almost-proof, and its gap as a predicate

This is the [DeansProblem.AlmostProof][] module of the [agda-lattices][] library.

[the paper]: https://arxiv.org/abs/YYMM.NNNNN

Freese's "Notes on Dean's problem" (1997) end with "an almost proof that an
ascending chain cannot exist".  In his words: "whenever the sublattice generated
by `a`, `b`, and `c` is contained [in] that generated by `a'`, `b'`, and `c'` and
both are free lattices, then the primed generators determine a truncation of the
term tree of `a`, `b`, and `c`.  This means that for each path of the tree there
is an element and an immediate subelement such that one of `a'`, `b'` or `c'`
lies in the interval between the element and the subelement.  There are only
finitely many possible truncations even counting the labelling.  Thus [if] we
had a[n] ascending chain `Sb(aᵏ, bᵏ, cᵏ)` of free sublattices we could assume
that the tru[n]cation pattern of `{a⁰, b⁰, c⁰}` determined by `{aᵏ, bᵏ, cᵏ}` is
the same for all `k`.  The next lemma almost shows this is impossible."  The lemma is his
Lemma 2 ([DeansProblem.Irredundant][]).  This module states the argument's three
pieces in Agda and the gap between them as a predicate, as follows.

1.  **The truncation** is [DeansProblem.Truncation][]: for a step
    `⟨σ⟩ ⊆ ⟨τ⟩` with `σ i = p i [ τ ]`, the canonical form of each `p i`
    truncates the canonical form of `σ i` (`truncation-canonical`{.AgdaFunction}).
    Every *cut* (a child `d` of a node `D` of the tree of `σ i`, lying below the
    member `τ j` for a generator child `j` of the matching node of the tree of
    `p i`, or dually above it) bounds a member of `τ` by elements of the tree of
    `σ`: `d ≤ τ j ≤ D` (`cut-belowᴶ`{.AgdaFunction}), or `D ≤ τ j ≤ d`
    (`cut-aboveᴹ`{.AgdaFunction}).

2.  **The box of a pattern**.  The *labelled pattern* of the step is the data
    the truncation records: the canonical forms of the terms `p i`, the node of
    the tree of `σ` each node of theirs matches, and the label `j` of each cut.
    Collecting the cuts gives the *box* of the pattern: for each `j`, `lo j` is
    the join of the cut children below `τ j` together with the meet nodes that
    have `j` as a meetand, and `hi j` is the meet of the cut children above
    `τ j` together with the join nodes that have `j` as a joinand.  The box is
    made of elements of the trees of `σ`, and every triple with the same
    labelled pattern over `σ` lies in it (`InBox`{.AgdaFunction}).  For a fixed
    base `σ` there are finitely many labelled patterns: the terms have bounded
    complexity (Theorem B of [the paper][], `complexity-mono`{.AgdaFunction}),
    and the node maps and labels are maps between finite sets.  So in an
    infinite
    chain over `σ` one labelled pattern recurs infinitely often, and all its
    links lie in one box.

3.  **Lemma 2 closes the argument at a strongly irredundant box**.  If one box
    is irredundant at `i` and holds two triples `σ'` and `τ'` with
    `⟨σ'⟩ ⊆ ⟨τ'⟩`, then `σ' i` lies in `⟨τ'⟩` and in the interval
    `[lo i, hi i]`, where Lemma 2 allows only `τ' i`: so `σ' i = τ' i`
    (`box-agree`{.AgdaFunction}).  A step has a fresh index, whose upper member
    is not in the lower sublattice, so no step has both its triples in a box
    irredundant at its fresh index (`no-step-in-box`{.AgdaFunction}), and in a
    chain at most one link lies in a strongly irredundant box
    (`at-most-one-link`{.AgdaFunction} of [DeansProblem.AlmostProof.Links][]).

**The gap** is the hypothesis of the third piece, `StronglyIrredundant`{.AgdaFunction}
for the box of the recurring pattern: nothing in the first two pieces provides
it.  If every labelled pattern that occurs twice in a chain had a strongly
irredundant box, Freese's argument would prove that no ascending chain exists,
and Dean's question would have a negative answer.  The predicate is decidable on
explicit boxes (`DecideBox.stronglyIrredundant?`{.AgdaFunction}), one Whitman
decision per condition, and it is monotone: narrowing a box keeps it
irredundant (`irredundantAt-narrow`{.AgdaFunction}), so a cheap wide box can
certify a narrow one.  [DeansProblem.Examples.FreeseBox][] decides it on Freese's
own step.

The second piece stays in prose, apart from its finiteness.  The pigeonhole on
the terms is that of Theorem C of [the paper][], Section 6, whose finite forms
[DeansProblem.Coherent][] and [DeansProblem.Coherent.Finite][] prove; the
pigeonhole on node maps and labels is a further finite choice of the same kind,
over the labelled patterns on the fixed trees of the base, which
[DeansProblem.Truncation.Pattern][] makes a type and lists completely.  The
theorems here are stated for an arbitrary box, so no type of patterns is
needed to state or use them.

#### Boxes in a free lattice

A *box* is two triples `lo` and `hi` of elements of `FL(X)`, the intervals
`[lo i, hi i]`; a triple `τ` lies in it (`InBox`{.AgdaFunction}) when
`lo i ≤ τ i ≤ hi i` for each `i`.  Irredundance at `i`
(`IrredundantAt`{.AgdaFunction}) and strong irredundance
(`StronglyIrredundant`{.AgdaFunction}) are Freese's conditions, stated with
Whitman's order so that they can be decided.

```agda
module _ {X : Type χ} where
  -- A triple in a box.
  InBox : (lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X) → Sub (Fin 3) X → Type χ
  InBox lo hi τ = ∀ i → lo i ≤ʷ τ i × τ i ≤ʷ hi i

  -- Freese's conditions at one index, and at all three.
  IrredundantAt : (lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X) → Fin 3 → Type χ
  IrredundantAt lo hi i = (lo i ≰ʷ (hi (other₁ i) ∨̇ hi (other₂ i)))
                        × ((lo (other₁ i) ∧̇ lo (other₂ i)) ≰ʷ hi i)

  StronglyIrredundant : (lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X) → Type χ
  StronglyIrredundant lo hi = ∀ i → IrredundantAt lo hi i
```

A narrower box is irredundant wherever a wider one is
(`irredundantAt-narrow`{.AgdaFunction}): raising the lower ends and lowering the
upper ends can only make the inequalities `lo i ≤ hi j ∨ hi k` and
`lo j ∧ lo k ≤ hi i` harder to satisfy.

```agda
  -- Narrowing a box keeps it irredundant.
  irredundantAt-narrow : {lo hi lo' hi' : Fin 3 → LatTerm X}
                       → (∀ j → lo j ≤ʷ lo' j) → (∀ j → hi' j ≤ʷ hi j)
                       → (i : Fin 3) → IrredundantAt lo hi i → IrredundantAt lo' hi' i
  irredundantAt-narrow lo≤ ≤hi i (lo≰ , ≰hi) =
    (λ p → lo≰ (≤ʷ-trans (lo≤ i) (≤ʷ-trans p (∨≤ (≤ʷ-trans (≤hi (other₁ i)) ∨̇-upperˡ)
                                                  (≤ʷ-trans (≤hi (other₂ i)) ∨̇-upperʳ)))))
    , (λ p → ≰hi (≤ʷ-trans (≤∧ (≤ʷ-trans ∧̇-lowerˡ (lo≤ (other₁ i)))
                                (≤ʷ-trans ∧̇-lowerʳ (lo≤ (other₂ i))))
                            (≤ʷ-trans p (≤hi i))))

  stronglyIrredundant-narrow : {lo hi lo' hi' : Fin 3 → LatTerm X}
                             → (∀ j → lo j ≤ʷ lo' j) → (∀ j → hi' j ≤ʷ hi j)
                             → StronglyIrredundant lo hi → StronglyIrredundant lo' hi'
  stronglyIrredundant-narrow lo≤ ≤hi si i = irredundantAt-narrow lo≤ ≤hi i (si i)
```

#### Lemma 2 in a free lattice

Lemma 2 in `FL(X)` (`pinnedᶠ`{.AgdaFunction}) is `lemma-2-at`{.AgdaFunction} of
[DeansProblem.Irredundant][] for the lattice `FL X`{.AgdaFunction}, whose order
`a ≤ b` (meaning `a ∧ b = a`) agrees with Whitman's (`≤ʷ→≤`{.AgdaFunction},
`≤→≤ʷ`{.AgdaFunction} of [Lattices.Free.Universal][]), and in which the value of
a term `t` at a triple `τ` is the substituted term `t [ τ ]`
(`⟦⟧-[]`{.AgdaFunction}).  If `τ` lies in a box irredundant at `i`, then `τ i` is
the only element of `⟨τ⟩` in `[lo i, hi i]`.

```agda
  -- Lemma 2 in FL(X), at one index.
  pinnedᶠ : (τ : Sub (Fin 3) X) {lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X} → InBox lo hi τ
          → (i : Fin 3) → IrredundantAt lo hi i
          → (t : LatTerm (Fin 3)) → lo i ≤ʷ t [ τ ] → t [ τ ] ≤ʷ hi i → t [ τ ] ≈ʷ τ i
  pinnedᶠ τ {lo} {hi} box i (lo≰ , ≰hi) t lo≤ ≤hi =
    subst (_≈ʷ τ i) (⟦⟧-[] τ t)
      (lemma-2-at (FL X) τ lo hi i ((λ p → lo≰ (≤→≤ʷ p)) , (λ p → ≰hi (≤→≤ʷ p)))
                  (λ j → ≤ʷ→≤ (proj₁ (box j)) , ≤ʷ→≤ (proj₂ (box j)))
                  t (≤ʷ→≤ (subst (lo i ≤ʷ_) (sym (⟦⟧-[] τ t)) lo≤))
                    (≤ʷ→≤ (subst (_≤ʷ hi i) (sym (⟦⟧-[] τ t)) ≤hi)))

  -- Freese's Lemma 2 in FL(X).
  lemma-2ᶠ : (τ : Sub (Fin 3) X) {lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X}
           → StronglyIrredundant lo hi → InBox lo hi τ → (i : Fin 3)
           → (t : LatTerm (Fin 3)) → lo i ≤ʷ t [ τ ] → t [ τ ] ≤ʷ hi i → t [ τ ] ≈ʷ τ i
  lemma-2ᶠ τ si box i = pinnedᶠ τ box i (si i)
```

#### Lemma 2 closes the argument at an irredundant box

Let `σ'` and `τ'` lie in one box, with each `σ' j` expressed in `τ'` by a term
`q j`, so that `⟨σ'⟩ ⊆ ⟨τ'⟩`.  At every index where the box is irredundant the
two triples agree (`box-agree`{.AgdaFunction}): `σ' i = q i [ τ' ]` lies in
`⟨τ'⟩` and in `[lo i, hi i]`, so it is `τ' i` by Lemma 2.  Neither triple need
freely generate.

```agda
  -- Two triples in one box, one inside the other's sublattice, agree where the box is irredundant.
  box-agree : {σ' τ' : Sub (Fin 3) X} (q : Sub (Fin 3) (Fin 3)) → (∀ j → σ' j ≈ʷ q j [ τ' ])
            → {lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X} → InBox lo hi σ' → InBox lo hi τ'
            → (i : Fin 3) → IrredundantAt lo hi i → σ' i ≈ʷ τ' i
  box-agree {σ'} {τ'} q σ'≈ boxσ' boxτ' i irr =
    ≈ʷ-trans (σ'≈ i) (pinnedᶠ τ' boxτ' i irr (q i)
                              (≤ʷ-trans (proj₁ (boxσ' i)) (proj₁ (σ'≈ i)))
                              (≤ʷ-trans (proj₂ (σ'≈ i)) (proj₂ (boxσ' i))))
```

A step `⟨σ⟩ ⊊ ⟨τ⟩` ([DeansProblem.Chain][]) has a fresh index `f`, whose member
`τ f` is not in `⟨σ⟩` (`fresh∉⟨σ⟩`{.AgdaFunction}).  If both triples lay in a
box irredundant at `f`, `box-agree`{.AgdaFunction} would give `σ f = τ f`, which
is in `⟨σ⟩` (`no-step-in-box`{.AgdaFunction}).  So a strongly irredundant box
holds the two triples of no step (`no-step-in-irredundant-box`{.AgdaFunction}),
and at most one link of any chain ([DeansProblem.AlmostProof.Links][]).

```agda
  -- No step has both triples in a box irredundant at its fresh index.
  no-step-in-box : (s : Step X) {lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X}
                 → InBox lo hi (Step.σ s) → InBox lo hi (Step.τ s)
                 → ¬ IrredundantAt lo hi (Step.fresh s)
  no-step-in-box s boxσ boxτ irr =
    fresh∉⟨σ⟩ s (ℊ fresh) (box-agree p σ≈p[τ] boxσ boxτ fresh irr)
    where open Step s

  -- No step has both triples in a strongly irredundant box.
  no-step-in-irredundant-box : (s : Step X) {lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X}
                             → StronglyIrredundant lo hi
                             → InBox lo hi (Step.σ s) → ¬ InBox lo hi (Step.τ s)
  no-step-in-irredundant-box s si boxσ boxτ = no-step-in-box s boxσ boxτ (si (Step.fresh s))
```

#### Deciding the predicate

With decidable equality of the generators, membership in a box and the
irredundance conditions are decided by Whitman's procedure, one decision per
inequality (the module `DecideBox`{.AgdaModule}).

```agda
module DecideBox {X : Type χ} (_≟_ : DecidableEquality X) where
  open Decision _≟_ using ( _≤ʷ?_ )

  -- Irredundance at one index, and at all three.
  irredundantAt? : (lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X) (i : Fin 3) → Dec (IrredundantAt lo hi i)
  irredundantAt? lo hi i = ¬? (lo i ≤ʷ? hi (other₁ i) ∨̇ hi (other₂ i))
                     ×-dec ¬? (lo (other₁ i) ∧̇ lo (other₂ i) ≤ʷ? hi i)

  stronglyIrredundant? : (lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X) → Dec (StronglyIrredundant lo hi)
  stronglyIrredundant? lo hi = all? (irredundantAt? lo hi)

  -- A triple in a box.
  inBox? : (lo hi : Fin 3 → LatTerm X) (τ : Sub (Fin 3) X) → Dec (InBox lo hi τ)
  inBox? lo hi τ = all? λ i → lo i ≤ʷ? τ i ×-dec τ i ≤ʷ? hi i
```

#### What remains

A proof of Dean's negative answer along Freese's lines needs the hypothesis this
module isolates: that the box of every labelled pattern occurring twice in a chain
over a common base is irredundant at some index fresh for the step between the two
links (strong irredundance, at all three indices, suffices).  Where it fails, at
some `i` either `lo i ≤ hi j ∨ hi k` or `lo j ∧ lo k ≤ hi i`: the cut children
that bound `τ i` from below all lie below the join of the upper bounds of the
other two members, or dually.  The bound of a meet node `D` with a meetand `j`
beside a generator meetand `k` is always harmless (`D ≤ τ k ≤ hi k`); the
substance is in the cut children.  [DeansProblem.Examples.FreeseBox][] records that
on Freese's own step the condition holds, with a generator of the ambient lattice
as the witness, and more: every triple that expresses his base by his terms lies
in one strongly irredundant box, so his terms express the base in at most one
link of any chain.  An infinite chain over his base must recur in other terms,
whose boxes fail the condition.

The predicate is stated on a box built from the trees of the base inside the
ambient free lattice, and it does not move inside `FL(3)`.  Read inside the
latest link `⟨τ⟩ ≅ FL(3)`, on the pair `(θ, M)` of Theorem C of [the paper][]
(Section 6; `θ` the transition from an earlier link, `M` the copy of the base
it fixes), the trees of the base are those of its terms in `FL(3)`, and the
cut children of the identity link are the generators of `FL(3)` themselves.
A generator `x` that occurs in those trees both as a joinand and as a meetand
gets the one-point interval `[x, x]`, and for such an `x` the condition that
the two links share a labelled pattern already says `θ(x) = x`, with no use of
Lemma 2 (`point-box-in-link`{.AgdaFunction} of
[DeansProblem.Truncation.Pattern][]).  The ambient cut children (Freese's `a₁`
below `a'`, which lies outside `⟨a', b', c'⟩` by Corollary 1.6,
`a₁∉⟨a',b',c'⟩`{.AgdaFunction} of [DeansProblem.Examples.FreeseGap][]) are
what make a box wider than a point, and they are visible only in the ambient
lattice, whose trees stay fixed along the chain.  So the gap is a property of a
recurring labelled pattern in the ambient lattice, as Freese stated it; the
pair `(θ, M)` carries the terms of the pattern and not its box.
