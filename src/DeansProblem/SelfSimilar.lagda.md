### No step toward Dean's problem is self-similar

This is the [DeansProblem.SelfSimilar][] module of the [agda-lattices][] library.

[the paper]: https://arxiv.org/abs/YYMM.NNNNN

A step `⟨σ⟩ ⊊ ⟨τ⟩` toward Dean's problem ([DeansProblem.Chain][]) is
*self-similar* if some injective endomorphism `Φ` of the ambient free lattice
sends `σ` to `τ`; iterating `Φ` would then give an infinite strictly ascending
chain of copies of `FL(3)` (`selfSimilar→chain`{.AgdaFunction}).  This module
proves that no step is self-similar (`no-self-similar-step`{.AgdaFunction}).
The search for such a `Φ` is therefore closed: for Freese's step the three
equations `Φ(a) = a'`, `Φ(b) = b'`, `Φ(c) = c'` have no injective solution
([DeansProblem.Examples.FreeseEquations][]).  These are Corollaries A1 and A2 of
[the paper][], Section 5.

The theorem is a statement about one free sublattice of finite rank and one
embedding (`self-similar-onto`{.AgdaFunction}).  Let `L` be the sublattice of
`FL(X)` freely generated by `τ₁, …, τₙ`, and let `Φ` be an injective
endomorphism of `FL(X)` whose image of `L` contains `L`: each `τᵢ` is
`θᵢ(Φτ₁, …, Φτₙ)` for some term `θᵢ` in `n` variables.  Then every generator of
`FL(n)` is, up to equality, one of the `θᵢ`, so `Φ` maps `L` onto `L`.  The proof
is as follows.

1.  **The orbit**.  Write `θ` also for the endomorphism of `FL(n)` with
    `θ(xᵢ) = θᵢ`, and `τ` for the embedding `FL(n) → FL(X)` with
    `τ(xᵢ) = τᵢ`.  The hypothesis says `τ = Φ ∘ τ ∘ θ`, and by induction
    `τ = Φᵏ ∘ τ ∘ θᵏ` for every `k` (`orbit`{.AgdaFunction}).  So `θ` is
    injective, since `τ` is (`θ-injective`{.AgdaFunction}).
2.  **Bounded complexity**.  A lattice embedding never lowers complexity
    ([Lattices.Free.Complexity][]), and `Φᵏ` and `τ` are embeddings, so
    `complexity (θᵏ(xⱼ)) ≤ complexity (τⱼ)` for every `k`
    (`orbit-bound`{.AgdaFunction}).
3.  **A repeat**.  Only finitely many elements of `FL(n)` have complexity at
    most `complexity (τⱼ)`, so `θᵃ(xⱼ) = θᵇ(xⱼ)` for some `a < b`
    (`orbit-repeats`{.AgdaFunction}).
4.  **The generator is in the image**.  Write `b = a + 1 + d`.  Then
    `θᵃ(xⱼ) = θᵃ(θ¹⁺ᵈ(xⱼ))`, and `θᵃ` is injective, so
    `xⱼ = θ¹⁺ᵈ(xⱼ) = θ(θᵈ(xⱼ))` lies in the image of `θ`.  By Corollary 1.6 of
    [Freese, Ježek, and Nation (1995)][] (`generator-in-image`{.AgdaFunction}),
    a generator in the sublattice generated by the `θᵢ` equals one of them.

For a step, `σ = θ ⨟ τ` with `θ` the step's terms `p`, and `Φ ∘ σ = τ` gives
`τ = Φ ∘ τ ∘ θ`; the theorem makes the step's fresh generator equal to one of
the `p i`, which the step forbids.  The decidable equality of the generators `X`
is needed only to compute canonical forms, and so complexities, in `FL(X)`; any
finite or countable set of generators has it.

What the argument shows about Dean's problem itself is weaker.  In any chain
`⟨σ₀⟩ ⊊ ⟨σ₁⟩ ⊊ ⋯`, the terms `Θₖ` expressing `σ₀` in `σₖ` (the composite of
the chain's steps) satisfy `complexity (Θₖ(xⱼ)) ≤ complexity (σ₀ j)` by the same
monotonicity, so they range over a finite set: this is Freese's observation that
`σ₀`'s canonical form is truncated by each `σₖ` in one of finitely many
patterns.  A self-similar chain has `Θₖ = θᵏ`, which commutes with `θ`, and that
is what turns a repeat into a contradiction; a general chain has no such
commutation, and the argument goes no further.  Theorem H of [the paper][]
(Section 8, [DeansProblem.NoChain][]) answers the problem by other means.

#### The orbit

Fix generators `X` with decidable equality, a family `τ` of `n` elements of
`FL(X)` that freely generates, an injective substitution `Φ` of `X` into itself,
and terms `θ` over `n` generators with `τ i = θ i [ τ ] [ Φ ]` for each `i`.
Substituting into an arbitrary term, `w [ τ ] = w [ θ ] [ τ ] [ Φ ]`
(`orbit-step`{.AgdaFunction}), and by induction
`w [ τ ] = w [ θᵏ ] [ τ ] [ Φᵏ ]` (`orbit`{.AgdaFunction}): the induction applies
the hypothesis to `w [ θ ]` and then `Φ` to both sides, and the iterates
reassociate by `[]-iter-suc`{.AgdaFunction} and `[]-iter-sucʳ`{.AgdaFunction}.
In particular `θ` is injective (`θ-injective`{.AgdaFunction}): equal images under
`θ` have equal images under `τ`, which is injective.

```agda
private
  -- An equation of lattice terms read as an equality in the free lattice.
  ≡→≈ʷ : {X : Type χ} {s t : LatTerm X} → s ≡ t → s ≈ʷ t
  ≡→≈ʷ {s = s} e = subst (s ≈ʷ_) e ≈ʷ-refl

module _ {X : Type χ} (_≟ˣ_ : DecidableEquality X) {n : ℕ}
         (τ : Sub (Fin n) X) (τ-free : FreelyGenerates τ)
         (Φ : Sub X X) (Φ-free : FreelyGenerates Φ)
         (θ : Sub (Fin n) (Fin n)) (τ≈ : ∀ i → τ i ≈ʷ θ i [ τ ] [ Φ ]) where

  private
    τ-inj : IsInjective (proj₁ (subst-hom τ))
    τ-inj = freelyGenerates→injective τ τ-free

    Φ-inj : IsInjective (proj₁ (subst-hom Φ))
    Φ-inj = freelyGenerates→injective Φ Φ-free

  -- One turn of the orbit.
  orbit-step : (w : LatTerm (Fin n)) → w [ τ ] ≈ʷ w [ θ ] [ τ ] [ Φ ]
  orbit-step w = subst (w [ τ ] ≈ʷ_) (trans ([]-⨟ w (θ ⨟ τ) Φ) (cong (_[ Φ ]) ([]-⨟ w θ τ)))
                       ([]-congʳ w τ≈)

  -- The k-th turn of the orbit.
  orbit : (k : ℕ) (w : LatTerm (Fin n)) → w [ τ ] ≈ʷ w [ iter θ k ] [ τ ] [ iter Φ k ]
  orbit zero     w = ≡→≈ʷ (sym (trans ([]-ℊ (w [ ℊ ] [ τ ])) (cong (_[ τ ]) ([]-ℊ w))))
  orbit (suc k)  w =
    ≈ʷ-trans (orbit-step w)
      (subst (w [ θ ] [ τ ] [ Φ ] ≈ʷ_)
             (trans (sym ([]-iter-sucʳ (w [ θ ] [ iter θ k ] [ τ ]) Φ k))
                    (cong (λ v → v [ τ ] [ iter Φ (suc k) ]) (sym ([]-iter-suc w θ k))))
             ([]-cong Φ (orbit k (w [ θ ]))))

  -- θ is injective.
  θ-injective : IsInjective (proj₁ (subst-hom θ))
  θ-injective {s} {t} e =
    τ-inj (≈ʷ-trans (orbit-step s) (≈ʷ-trans ([]-cong Φ ([]-cong τ e)) (≈ʷ-sym (orbit-step t))))
```

#### Bounded complexity and a repeat

Complexity in `FL(n)` uses the decidable equality of `Fin n`.  Along the orbit of
a generator `ℊ j` under `θ`, complexity stays at most that of `τ j`
(`orbit-bound`{.AgdaFunction}): `θᵏ(xⱼ)` has at most the complexity of its image
under the embedding `τ`, which has at most the complexity of its image under the
embedding `Φᵏ`, which is `τ j` by the orbit.  So the orbit lies in the finite list
`bounded`{.AgdaFunction} of [Lattices.Free.Complexity][], and by the pigeonhole
principle it repeats (`orbit-repeats`{.AgdaFunction}).

```agda
  -- The orbit of a generator has bounded complexity.
  orbit-bound : (j : Fin n) (k : ℕ) → complexity _≟_ (iter θ k j) ≤ complexity _≟ˣ_ (τ j)
  orbit-bound j k =
    ≤-trans (complexity-mono _≟_ _≟ˣ_ τ τ-inj (iter θ k j))
      (≤-trans (complexity-mono _≟ˣ_ _≟ˣ_ (iter Φ k) (iter-injective Φ Φ-inj k) (iter θ k j [ τ ]))
               (≤-reflexive (sym (complexity-resp _≟ˣ_ (orbit k (ℊ j))))))

  -- The orbit of a generator repeats.
  orbit-repeats : (j : Fin n) → Σ[ a ∈ ℕ ] Σ[ b ∈ ℕ ] a < b × iter θ a j ≈ʷ iter θ b j
  orbit-repeats j =
    pigeonhole-≈ (bounded (complexity _≟ˣ_ (τ j))) (λ k → iter θ k j)
                 (λ k → bounded-complete (complexity _≟ˣ_ (τ j)) (iter θ k j) (orbit-bound j k))
    where open Bounded (_≟_ {n}) (allFin n) ∈-allFin using ( bounded ; bounded-complete )
```

#### The theorem

From a repeat `θᵃ(xⱼ) = θᵇ(xⱼ)` with `b = (1 + d) + a`, the iterates add
(`[]-iter-+`{.AgdaFunction}): `θᵇ(xⱼ)` is `θᵃ` applied to `θ¹⁺ᵈ(xⱼ)`.  Injectivity
of `θᵃ` gives `xⱼ = θ¹⁺ᵈ(xⱼ)`, which is `θ` applied to `θᵈ(xⱼ)`
(`[]-iter-sucʳ`{.AgdaFunction}), so `xⱼ` lies in the sublattice the `θ i`
generate, and Corollary 1.6 makes it equal to one of them
(`self-similar-onto`{.AgdaFunction}).

```agda
  -- Every generator of FL(n) is one of the terms θ i: Φ maps ⟨τ⟩ onto itself.
  self-similar-onto : (j : Fin n) → Σ[ i ∈ Fin n ] ℊ j ≈ʷ θ i
  self-similar-onto j =
    let (a , b , a<b , e) = orbit-repeats j
        (d , a+d≡b) = m≤n⇒∃[o]m+o≡n a<b
        b≡ : b ≡ suc d + a
        b≡ = trans (sym a+d≡b) (cong suc (+-comm a d))
        e' : ℊ j [ iter θ a ] ≈ʷ iter θ (suc d) j [ iter θ a ]
        e' = subst (ℊ j [ iter θ a ] ≈ʷ_)
                   (trans (cong (λ m → iter θ m j) b≡) ([]-iter-+ (ℊ j) θ (suc d) a)) e
        x≈ : ℊ j ≈ʷ iter θ d j [ θ ]
        x≈ = subst (ℊ j ≈ʷ_) ([]-iter-sucʳ (ℊ j) θ d)
                   (iter-injective θ θ-injective a {ℊ j} {iter θ (suc d) j} e')
    in generator-in-image θ (iter θ d j) x≈
```

For a step `⟨σ⟩ ⊊ ⟨τ⟩` and `Φ` with `Φ ∘ σ = τ`, the hypothesis of the theorem
holds with `θ` the step's terms `p`: `τ i = σ i [ Φ ] = p i [ τ ] [ Φ ]`.  The
theorem then makes the step's fresh generator one of the `p i`, contradicting
the step (`no-self-similar-step`{.AgdaFunction}).

```agda
-- No step toward Dean's problem is self-similar.
no-self-similar-step : {X : Type χ} → DecidableEquality X → (s : Step X)
                     → (Φ : Sub X X) → FreelyGenerates Φ → ¬ (∀ i → Step.σ s i [ Φ ] ≈ʷ Step.τ s i)
no-self-similar-step _≟ˣ_ s Φ Φ-free Φσ≈τ =
  let (i , e) = self-similar-onto _≟ˣ_ τ τ-free Φ Φ-free p τ≈ fresh in fresh∉p i e
  where
  open Step s

  -- The step's upper family, through Φ and the step's terms.
  τ≈ : ∀ i → τ i ≈ʷ p i [ τ ] [ Φ ]
  τ≈ i = ≈ʷ-trans (≈ʷ-sym (Φσ≈τ i)) ([]-cong Φ (σ≈p[τ] i))
```
